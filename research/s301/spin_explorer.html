Astrometric imprint, true scale

S301 around Sgr A*, from exact Kerr geodesics (KerrGeoPy): a = 83 mas, e = 0.9832, r_p = 136 R_S, P = 8.69 yr, orientation 1 of Abd El Dayem et al. (2026). The chart and the readouts are the sky offset between the orbit around the spinning black hole and the orbit around a non-spinning one with the same elements, at the same emission time — the star itself does not spin. The sky track instead pairs the two orbits at equal orbital phase, so the exaggeration magnifies the change in the orbit’s shape and not the star’s roughly hour-long earlier arrival at pericentre. The offset is a quadratic polynomial in the spin vector (χ cos ξ, χ sin ξ cos λ, −χ sin ξ sin λ) in the orbit frame (normal, pericentre, third axis), fitted to 78 exact geodesics and tested on 40 held-out ones: the worst error over 2017–2045 is 0.01 % of each case’s peak signal for the chart (median <0.01 %, never more than 0.1 µas) and 0.01 % for the sky track. The azimuth λ follows the convention of the s301_run.py script. The sky track is drawn with the displacement multiplied so it is visible; the chart and the readouts are not exaggerated. The offsets are at fixed orbital elements, not refitted: both orbits share the pericentre epoch (2023.13), so the offset is zero there by construction, and a real fit would absorb part of the signal — read them as the raw spin signal. The pericentre advance and tilt rows are the orbit-averaged 1.5PN estimates. An earlier version of this page approximated the spin by kicks at pericentre and underestimated the signal by about 20 %; this one replaces it.
